Apollo M-1 · 64 × 64

Arcade

A physical LED matrix on the wall. It started as a games console — 14 of them, each with a self-playing auto mode — and grew into a live information panel: aircraft overhead, the next ISS pass, scores across every sport ESPN covers, weather with severe-alert takeover, headlines, and a full-panel Game Day mode for one event. Driven by a service on the home network, streaming frames to the panel over DDP.

Control Panel

The full desktop dashboard — pick a game, steer with your keyboard, tweak backgrounds and mirror settings. Works on the home network.

Open Control Panel

Phone Remote

Hold it sideways like a game controller. Browse and pick games right on the panel — the phone is just the D-pad and buttons. Home WiFi only.

Live data

Real sources, no invented numbers — if a feed is down it says so rather than showing something false.

Clock

The resting state: time, date, temperature, next ISS pass, sunrise.

Crypto and stocks on a configurable watchlist. Keyless sources.

ISS

Flights

ADS-B traffic overhead, plane icon turned to the real heading, and a notable-aircraft flag for heavies, helicopters and emergency squawks.

Sports

Every sport ESPN is covering right now — from MLB and the WNBA to golf, tennis, soccer and MMA. Leagues with nothing on simply don't appear. Select any event to expand it into full detail, or pin a team for a live score with outs and baserunners, down and distance.

News

Headlines from any RSS feed you point it at. Titles only.

Weather

NWS conditions, today's high/low and sunrise. Severe alerts take over whatever is on screen.

Guestbook

Messages visitors leave on this site, shown on the wall.

Game Day

A different kind of mode: opt-in, one event, and nothing else competes for the panel while it runs.

The only thing happening tonight

Point it at a UFC card or at your team’s game and it takes the whole panel. For a card it tracks where the night stands, who is walking out next with their records, and the moment a fight ends it throws the result up as a highlight — method, round and time, big. Fight statistics too: significant strikes, takedowns, control time. It hands the panel back on its own when the event is over. Only a severe weather alert can interrupt it.

The rest

The parts that tie it together.

Ambient rotation

Cycles the data modes as curated “channel idents” — one fact each, its own colour and entrance, skipping anything with nothing to say.

Browse anything

Most panels like this are push-only. Tap to step, hold to fast-scroll — the same control in every mode, and over the rotation itself.

Night dimming

Scheduled day/night brightness with a smooth fade, applied to every mode. Severe alerts stay bright.

Video · Mirror · Cast

Loop a file, mirror a window, or cast from your phone.

Self-play

Every game has an auto mode — leave it on as living wall art.

Hand-back

Turn it off and the panel returns to normal WLED lighting.
